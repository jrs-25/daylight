# Architecture Decision Records — Daylight

*This document captures key architectural and product design decisions made during the development of Daylight. Each record follows the standard ADR format: context, decision, rationale, alternatives considered, and consequences.*

---

## ADR-001: Patient-Facing Intake Over Clinical Decision Support

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
The original prototype design was a clinician-facing risk screening tool that assembled patient vignettes from structured EHR data and surfaced signals to providers. This addressed the problem of missed diagnoses in primary care but assumed the patient had already engaged with the healthcare system.

**Decision:**
Reorient the product as a patient-facing intake experience designed for people who have not yet sought care.

**Rationale:**
The more upstream barrier to mental health care is the commitment threshold — the decision to seek help at all. Existing tools (screeners, intake forms, provider directories) assume this decision has already been made. The unmet need is earlier in the journey: a private, low-stakes space where someone can explore what they have been experiencing before they are ready to engage with a clinician.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Retain the clinician-facing design and build a patient portal alongside it — rejected as scope-doubling with unclear priority
- Build a hybrid tool serving both audiences — rejected as producing a product with no clear primary user

**Consequences:**
- The output is no longer a clinical risk flag; it is a profile that enables a provider match
- HIPAA surface area changes significantly — no EHR integration, but patient-generated data requires careful handling in production
- The evaluation criteria shift from sensitivity/specificity to engagement quality, completion rate, and match accuracy

## ADR-002: Anonymous Session Design — No Name Collected

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
Standard intake flows collect identifying information (name, date of birth, contact details) at the start of the session. This is necessary for provider matching and insurance billing but creates friction for users who are not yet committed to seeking care.

**Decision:**
No name or identifying information is collected from the hero at any point. A UUID session token is generated by the system and serves as the sole identifier.

**Rationale:**
A user who is not ready to admit they need help is not ready to put their name on it. Requiring identification at the start raises the commitment threshold at precisely the moment the product is trying to lower it. The session token preserves the ability to link sessions in production without requiring the user to self-identify.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Optional name field — rejected because optional fields still signal expectation; their presence affects behavior
- Pseudonym/handle — rejected as unnecessary complexity with no material benefit over a generated token

**Consequences:**
- Provider matching in production must be triggered by user choice after the session, not pre-populated from intake data
- Session continuity across visits requires the user to retain their token — a known UX challenge deferred to production
- The vignette stored in the backend contains no PII by design

## ADR-003: Fixed Topic Arc With Adaptive Depth

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
Two approaches to conversation structure were considered: a fixed sequence of questions with no adaptation, and a fully AI-driven conversation where topic selection and order are determined by the model based on prior responses.

**Decision:**
Use a fixed seven-topic arc that the system is committed to completing, with Claude controlling pacing, tone, and follow-up depth within each topic.

**Rationale:**
A fully scripted sequence would miss moments when a disclosure warrants deeper exploration. A fully AI-driven sequence risks skipping clinically significant topics if the user deflects early — particularly the elevated states topic, which users rarely volunteer without prompting. The hybrid gives the system accountability for coverage and the model autonomy over texture.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Fully scripted sequence — rejected for rigidity and poor handling of deflection
- Fully AI-driven topic selection from a defined space — rejected due to unpredictable coverage and harder evaluation
- Weighted topic selection (model chooses from remaining topics by priority) — deferred as a production refinement

**Consequences:**
- All seven topics are guaranteed to be addressed in every complete session
- Claude may spend more turns on one topic than another based on what surfaces — session length varies
- Evaluation of conversation quality requires assessing both coverage (did all topics get addressed?) and depth (did the model probe appropriately?)

## ADR-004: Elevated States as a Distinct Topic

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
Most mental health screeners focus on depressive symptoms. Bipolar disorder requires surfacing hypomanic and manic signals — periods of elevated energy, reduced need for sleep, inflated confidence, or racing thoughts — which users frequently do not identify as symptoms because they are experienced as positive states.

**Decision:**
Elevated states are treated as a distinct topic in the conversation arc, asked without using clinical terminology (bipolar, hypomania, mania) unless the hero introduces those terms first.

**Rationale:**
Users experiencing elevated states rarely present them as problems. Framing the question around the experience ("periods when you felt unusually energized or excited, when others didn't seem to match your energy") rather than the clinical label increases the likelihood of accurate disclosure. Burying this question inside a general mood topic risks it being skipped when the conversation is dominated by depressive presentation.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Integrate elevated state questions into the mood and affect topic — rejected because it deprioritizes a signal that is easy to miss
- Ask directly about bipolar disorder — rejected because the label triggers defensiveness and is not the system's role to apply

**Consequences:**
- The conversation explicitly avoids diagnostic language for this topic, which requires careful system prompt design
- The vignette must capture elevated state signals distinctly from depressive signals for provider interpretation
- Evaluation cases must include presentations where elevated states are the primary signal

## ADR-005: Two-Mode Crisis Detection

**Status:** Accepted — thresholds require clinical review before production

**Context:**
Mental health intake conversations will surface disclosures about death, hopelessness, and wanting to disappear. Standard clinical escalation protocols treat any such disclosure as a potential crisis requiring immediate intervention. This approach causes users to self-censor, producing less accurate pictures of their experience.

**Decision:**
Implement two distinct response modes based on the nature of the disclosure:

- **Reflective disclosure mode:** The user is sharing an experience, not expressing immediate intent. The system responds with warmth, normalizes the disclosure, and continues the conversation.
- **Acute distress mode:** Language suggests possible immediate risk. The conversation stops, the system responds directly, and crisis resources (988) are foregrounded.

**Rationale:**
Thinking about death is not equivalent to suicidal ideation. Conflating the two teaches users that certain thoughts are unsayable in this context, which degrades the quality of the intake and leaves the therapist vignette incomplete. The distinction between reflective and acute disclosure is clinically meaningful and should be encoded in the product.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Single escalation mode for all death-related disclosures — rejected as the primary approach; creates self-censorship
- No escalation logic — rejected; the system has an obligation to respond to genuine acute distress
- Defer all crisis logic to a human review layer — not feasible in a real-time conversational system

**Consequences:**
- The specific language thresholds for triggering acute distress mode are a known open question requiring clinical input before production; this is explicitly documented
- False negatives (missing genuine crisis) carry greater harm than false positives; the prototype errs conservative
- 988 is displayed in the UI at all times regardless of conversation state, as a baseline safety measure
- Evaluation must include cases that test both modes

## ADR-006: Community Enrichment as Invisible Context

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
Neighborhood-level socioeconomic and mental health access data is publicly available and provides meaningful context for understanding a user's experience of isolation and barriers to care. The question is how this data should surface in the product.

**Decision:**
Use County Health Rankings data to classify the hero's county into one of three context types (high distress, provider shortage, default). This classification informs the tone of the system's responses — specifically how it frames "you are not alone" messaging — but is never surfaced as a statistic or data point to the hero.

**Rationale:**
The purpose of this data is to make the system's empathic framing accurate rather than generic. A hero in a county with very high rates of mental health distress and few providers has a meaningfully different experience of isolation than someone in a well-served urban area. The system should know this and speak accordingly. Citing the statistic directly would shift the register from empathic to informational, which is not the role this product plays.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Area Deprivation Index (ADI) by zip code — considered; captures socioeconomic deprivation but is less specific to mental health than County Health Rankings
- No external enrichment — rejected; loses meaningful contextual signal that is freely available
- Surfacing the data to the hero directly — rejected as inconsistent with product orientation

**Consequences:**
- The enrichment layer must fail gracefully when zip code is not provided or not found; no error surfaces to the hero
- County Health Rankings is an annual release; the data file must be versioned and updated
- Zip-to-county crosswalk introduces a dependency (HUD USPS crosswalk file)

## ADR-007: Single Model Over Multi-Model Ensemble

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
The original architecture used a two-model ensemble (Claude + Gemini) running in parallel, with disagreement between models treated as a clinically meaningful confidence signal, grounded in Lopez et al. (Stanford 2025) research on complementary behavioral profiles.

**Decision:**
Use a single Claude model. The ensemble is dropped.

**Rationale:**
The ensemble's value was in surfacing disagreement as a signal in a physician-facing screening tool. In a patient-facing conversational system, there is no mechanism to expose model disagreement to the hero without undermining the conversation. The added complexity — dual API integration, async orchestration, agreement scoring — does not produce a user-facing benefit in this product context. The clinical differentiator in Daylight is the conversation design and the demographic enrichment layer, not inter-model agreement.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Retain ensemble for backend vignette scoring only — rejected as complexity without proportionate benefit at prototype stage
- Use ensemble for crisis detection specifically — deferred as a production refinement worth evaluating

**Consequences:**
- Significantly reduced implementation complexity
- Single API dependency (Anthropic only)
- Model is pinned explicitly (`claude-sonnet-4-6`) for auditability
- The vignette does not carry a confidence score derived from model agreement; confidence is expressed through the completeness and depth of topic coverage

## ADR-008: Vignette as the Matching Interface

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
The prototype does not build a dynamic therapist matching system. The question is how to architect the backend so that matching is an extension rather than a rebuild.

**Decision:**
Every completed session produces a structured vignette — a topic-by-topic summary of what the hero shared, with key signals extracted — stored in SQLite. This vignette is the interface between the intake system and a future matching system.

**Rationale:**
The vignette answers what a matching system needs to know: what has this person been experiencing, what signals emerged, what barriers to care do they have, and what would make them trust a provider. Designing the vignette as a first-class output — rather than a log or side effect — means the matching system can be built against a stable contract.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Store raw conversation transcript only — rejected; too unstructured for matching without significant processing
- Build a lightweight matching prototype alongside the intake — rejected as scope expansion that dilutes focus on the intake experience

**Consequences:**
- The vignette schema is a contract; changes to it require versioning
- The final vignette generation call (summarizing the full conversation) adds latency at session close
- Provider view in the app renders the vignette, giving evaluators a concrete picture of what a therapist would receive

## ADR-009: Scope Boundaries for Prototype

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
A production mental health platform of this kind involves session persistence, user accounts, provider matching, insurance verification, scheduling, clinical supervision, and regulatory compliance. Scope must be bounded for the prototype to be buildable and evaluable.

**Decision:**
The prototype explicitly excludes: session persistence across visits, user authentication, dynamic provider matching, conditions beyond depression and bipolar disorder, and HIPAA-compliant infrastructure.

**Rationale:**
The prototype's purpose is to validate the core product hypothesis — that a warm, adaptive, anonymous intake conversation can build a clinically meaningful picture of a hero's experience — and to demonstrate the architecture that would support production features. Each excluded item is documented as a known extension, not a gap.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Include basic session persistence via local storage — rejected; adds complexity and creates false expectations about production-readiness
- Expand condition coverage to anxiety — deferred; the topic arc and system prompt would need meaningful extension to handle anxiety presentations with the same rigor as depression and bipolar

**Consequences:**
- The prototype is explicitly a single-session experience
- All deferred features are named in documentation so evaluators understand what is and is not built
- The architecture must make extensions obvious without building them

## ADR-010: Anchor Topic Coverage in Validated Screening Constructs, Not Their Items

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**
The seven-topic arc from SPEC.md gave the model a list of subjects but left "genuinely covered" to its judgment. In use, this produced warm conversations whose depth varied turn to turn, and vignettes a therapist could not reliably map to anything they would ask themselves. The obvious fix — having the model administer PHQ-9 / MDQ — would turn the conversation back into the form the product exists to avoid.

**Decision:**
Each topic carries a `listen_for` list of constructs drawn from validated instruments: PHQ-9 (mood and affect), MDQ including its two gate questions — co-occurrence and functional impact — (elevated states), the C-SSRS screener ladder (difficult thoughts), standard family psychiatric history, AUDIT-C plus sleep/appetite history (lifestyle), and MI change-talk (relationship to help). The list is rendered into the per-turn `session_state` block and into the vignette prompt. The system prompt names the conversational stance as motivational interviewing (OARS, rolling with resistance) and the crisis threshold as the C-SSRS ideation/intent distinction — ideation, burden, hopelessness, and past attempts are disclosures; intent, plan, means, or goodbye framing escalate.

**Rationale:**
The instruments define what a clinician needs to have heard. The conversation defines how it gets heard. Separating the two lets the companion stay a companion while the vignette becomes something a therapist recognizes, and gives `topic_status: complete` a criterion that can be evaluated rather than felt.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- Administer the instruments verbatim — rejected; it is exactly the commitment threshold the product lowers
- Leave coverage to model judgment and tune by eval — rejected; produces no shared vocabulary with the provider side
- Score the instruments from the transcript post hoc — deferred; may be worth adding to the vignette later, but a computed PHQ-9 on a non-administered instrument invites false precision

**Consequences:**
- `TOPICS` stays verbatim to the spec; `LISTEN_FOR` sits beside it and is the thing to review clinically
- The vignette now distinguishes "hero said no" from "not asked," which the provider view should surface
- The `safety.py` rule layer still needs clinical review; this ADR changes the model-side threshold language only, and keeps it consistent with the rule layer's existing triggers
- Live eval (`run_evals.py --live`) should be re-run to check topic coverage and signal extraction did not regress under the stricter completion criterion
